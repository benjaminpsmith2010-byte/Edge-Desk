How to read the numbers

  1. Market is the average of every market signal on hand: the data feed's pregame win probability, FanDuel's price with its margin removed, and the multi-book consensus price with its margin removed.
  2. Model is a record-only rating: each team's win rate, pulled toward .500 by adding 11 average games (NFL) or 12 (NBA), compared head to head with a home edge. It knows nothing about injuries, quarterbacks or rest.
  3. Fair blends the two in log-odds at the market weight you set. When the model and the market disagree by a lot, the usual cause is news the record does not contain.
  4. Value appears when the fair win chance beats the price’s break-even chance by 2 points or more. Smaller gaps are inside the error of any estimate. Stake is Kelly × your multiple, capped at your max.
  5. Where there is no feed number, enter both sides' prices from the sharpest book you can find in the Odds lab to get a no-vig line, then put that in the card's own-% box.

Enter the same market from several books. The lab strips out each book's margin, averages the no-vig lines into a consensus, finds the best price per outcome, and checks for an arbitrage. American (−110, +145) or decimal (1.91) both work.

Tennis can be priced from one statistic per player: the share of points they win on their own serve. The pricer chains points into games, tiebreaks, sets and the match, for best of three or five, before the match or from a live score. Use each player's serve-points-won % on the surface in question, adjusted for the opponent's return if you have it.

The starting numbers are placeholders, not real players. The model assumes every point on a player's serve is equally likely to be won, with a tiebreak at 6–6 in every set.

The same bet placed hundreds of times, 2,000 simulated seasons. Set the win rate you believe you have and the win rate you really have. Stakes follow what you believe; results follow what is true. The gap between those two numbers is where most bankrolls go.

Shaded bands hold the middle 50% and middle 90% of simulated runs. The line is the median run.

Record every bet, then add the closing price once the game starts. Beating the closing price on average is the best evidence that your picks have an edge, and it shows up long before profit does.
